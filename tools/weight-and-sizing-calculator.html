Weight & Sizing Calculator

Three linked checks for Phase 03 sizing: a running weight-and-CG table, hover thrust margin, and wing loading. Everything computes live as you type, in your browser only — nothing is sent anywhere.

01 · Weight & CG

Weight & center-of-gravity table

Pick your own reference point — the nose is the usual choice — and measure every component's distance from it consistently. This tool doesn't need to know which point you chose; it only needs those distances to be measured from the same point every time. CG position is computed with the standard moment method: CG = Σ(weighti × distancei) ÷ Σ(weighti). See Structures & Materials's "Weight and CG budgeting" section for why this matters and why a physical balance check before first flight still beats trusting the spreadsheet alone. Rows are saved in this browser's local storage, so they'll survive a reload here — but won't follow you to another device.

02 · Thrust margin (hover)

Hover thrust-to-weight ratio

Matches the sizing rule already in Phase 03 of the build manual and the Component Selection Guide's Motors section: total thrust across all motors should be at least 2× all-up weight, for margin against wind, maneuvering, and battery sag. Below 2× is flagged marginal, and below 1.5× is flagged insufficient — same thresholds, applied live to your own numbers.

03 · Wing loading

The qualitative bands below are synthesized from real RC-design references, not a single universal standard — treat them as an informed approximation, and see the citations under the page for exactly where each number comes from.

Where the wing-loading bands come from
  • RC Wing Loading Calculator — codingace.net — a three-tier example (trainer ~32 g/dm² "light and forgiving," sport ~53 g/dm² "moderate and responsive," scale warbird ~76 g/dm² "fast and demanding") that the bands above are built around.
  • Trainer Design — University of Notre Dame AME 40462 course notes (PDF) — gives ~60 g/dm² as a typical value for a 60"-span trainer, and notes wing loading needs to run lower on smaller models — consistent with, and one more data point behind, the bands above.
