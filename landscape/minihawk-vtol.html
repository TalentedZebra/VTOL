MiniHawk VTOL

An 800mm open-source tilt-rotor tricopter with a plank-style flying wing — one of the earliest and most-referenced designs in this exact configuration, and the design this project's own build manual cites as its closest open-source precedent.

MiniHawk's own repository describes its layout directly: "the forward pair tilting for forward flight and yaw control, and the rear motor fixed for hover only" — the same 2-tilt-plus-1-fixed configuration surveyed across this whole section.

Configuration

Two front motors (2207/2306-class, roughly 2500kV) tilt via a servo linkage between vertical and horizontal; one rear motor (also 2207/2306-class, roughly 2000kV) stays fixed and runs only in hover.

Airframe & body design

800mm wingspan, roughly 1000–1200g all-up weight (the bare airframe alone is about 460g). The wing is a "plank"-style flying wing with internal wire conduits routed through the structure, living-hinge elevons molded directly into the wing rather than hinged separately, and a carbon-fiber spar for bending stiffness. Construction is FDM 3D printing in PLA.

What's distinctive

The plank-wing-with-living-hinge approach is a notably different structural strategy from the Stallion's more conventional wing-plus-separate-control-surface layout, and MiniHawk is smaller and lighter than most of the other designs surveyed here — closer to this project's own ~700–900mm target class than the Stallion is.

Flight controller & software

ArduPlane, typically on an mRo PixRacer Pro or a Matek F405/F765-WING board. The project also has a Betaflight-VTOL branch, though ArduPlane appears to be the primary, more actively documented path.

Cost

No stated total build cost. Based on the parts list alone, electronics run roughly $300–500 — an approximate inference, not a figure MiniHawk's own documentation states directly, so confirm current part prices before budgeting from it.
