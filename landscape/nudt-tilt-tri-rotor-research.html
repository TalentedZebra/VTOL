NUDT tilt tri-rotor UAV research line

A multi-paper Chinese academic research program (National University of Defense Technology) studying tilt tri-rotor modeling, attitude control, and hover-to-cruise mode conversion — a sustained research effort rather than a single one-off paper.

This entry covers a line of related work rather than one paper. The best-documented single source, independently fetched and read in full, is Chen, Zhang, Wang, Shen & Li's 2021 SAGE paper on conversion control; other papers in the same line (Yu & Zhang 2017, an earlier Chen et al. 2017 flight-test paper, and a related Springer book chapter) are referenced here but weren't all independently confirmed in full — see the note on flight-test claims below.

Configuration

Three fixed-pitch rotors: two front rotors tilt from −20° to 90° via servo actuation, and one tail rotor stays fixed. Control allocation is explicit in the 2021 paper: roll comes from differential thrust between the two front rotors, and yaw comes from differential tilt angle between them — the fixed rear rotor's role is thrust/force compensation rather than directly generating roll or yaw.

Airframe & body design

Per the 2021 paper's own specification table: maximum weight 6.89kg, 1.8m wingspan, 1.6m length, 0.4m² wing area, 20 m/s cruise speed. No construction-material details were found.

What's distinctive

This is the heaviest and largest design surveyed in this section by a wide margin (6.89kg vs. 2–3kg for the Stallion, the next largest) and is explicitly framed around modeling uncertainty and robust conversion control — the 2021 paper's stated contribution is control design that specifically handles unmodeled dynamics during the hover-to-cruise transition, not just baseline attitude control.

Flight controller & software

Research-specific flight-control software described in the papers themselves; not a named commercial or open-source firmware project.

Cost

Research prototype — no public pricing.

A note on flight-test claims

The 2021 SAGE paper's own results are simulation-only (its reported conversion-mode duration and deviation figures come from simulation, not flight data) — it should not be cited as flight-tested. A separate, earlier paper in the same research line is explicitly titled "Control and flight test of a tilt-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle" (Chen, Zhang, Zhang & Shen, 2017), which likely is the flight-tested work, but its abstract/full text couldn't be loaded during this research (the journal page redirected to its homepage). Treat any flight-test claim for this research line as attributable to that 2017 paper specifically, and unconfirmed until someone reads it directly.

Sources

  • Control and flight test of a tilt-rotor unmanned aerial vehicle — Chen, Zhang, Zhang & Shen, 2017 (flight-test claim not independently confirmed — page did not load during this research)
