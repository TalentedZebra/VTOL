Flightory Stallion VTOL

A 3D-printed, open-source tilt-rotor tricopter at a notably larger scale than most hobby VTOL builds — and the design this project itself is using as its own v1 reference build.

This entry is a neutral profile of the design itself. For this project's own reasoning on why it was chosen as our v1 build (vs. a fully custom or semi-custom path), see Reference Design: Flightory Stallion VTOL in Research & Principles — that page covers the build-vs-custom tradeoffs; this one just describes what the Stallion is.

Configuration

Flightory's own product page describes the VTOL conversion as a "tricopter layout design with front tilt and a rear stationary motor" — two wingtip motors tilt on servos between vertical and horizontal, and one fixed rear motor runs only in hover for yaw authority and extra lift.

Airframe & body design

1340mm wingspan, 990mm length, 1500–3000g all-up weight for the base airframe (2000–3000g once converted to VTOL). The wing uses an Eppler E205 airfoil with a V-tail on a single tail boom, and the whole structure is 3D-printed in LW-PLA and PETG. Stated cruise speed is 60–70 km/h at 0–2° angle of attack, with the wing's CG placed 60mm from the leading edge for what Flightory describes as zero pitching moment at zero angle of attack — a no-trim cruise design.

What's distinctive

Scale is the standout feature: at 1340mm and 2–3kg, it's substantially larger than most of the other tricopter tilt-rotor designs surveyed here (MiniHawk at 800mm, the E-flite Convergence at 650mm). Flightory states the design is CFD-validated and flight-tested, and recommends specific hardware for the VTOL conversion: Emax ECO II 2807 1300KV or T-Motor F90 1300KV motors, Kingmax 1203MD or GDW DS041MG tilt servos, and BLHeli_S 45A or Lumenier 51A ESCs.

Flight controller & software

SpeedyBee F405 Wing running ArduPilot (per background reporting on the build; not stated on Flightory's own product pages).

Cost

Files: $49.98 (base airframe $34.99 + VTOL conversion pack $14.99). Electronics using Flightory's recommended hardware run roughly $350–500+, though a complete parts-and-filament total wasn't independently verified — confirm current prices before ordering.
