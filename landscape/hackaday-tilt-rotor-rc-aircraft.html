Hackaday Tilt Rotor VTOL RC Aircraft

A scratch-built tilt-rotor tricopter built from foam board and bamboo skewers on top of an existing Flite Test flying-wing airframe — proof that this configuration doesn't require 3D printing or composite construction to work.

A note on sourcing for this entry: the project's own Hackaday.io page returned a server error on every fetch attempt across this research (three attempts total), so the details below come from consistent, independently-repeated web search results rather than a direct read of the source page itself. Try loading the page directly before treating any of this as final.

Configuration

Search-indexed descriptions of the project consistently state: the front two rotors tilt a full 90° between hover and forward flight, tilting roughly 5° differentially from each other in hover for yaw control; the rear rotor is fixed and is turned off entirely once the aircraft is in forward flight.

Airframe & body design

The airframe combines a flying-wing/lifting-body shape with the tricopter motor layout, built on an existing Flite Test flying-wing design as its starting point. Construction is paper-lined foam board reinforced with bamboo BBQ skewers, held together with zip ties and hot glue, with a 3D-printed firewall for the motor mounts. No wingspan or weight figures were found.

What's distinctive

This is the least expensive airframe construction method of any design surveyed here by a wide margin — foam board is a common, very low-cost hobby material, in sharp contrast to the 3D-printed or composite construction of every other entry in this section. It's a useful data point that the tilt-rotor tricopter configuration itself doesn't demand a particular fabrication method.

Flight controller & software

Described only as "custom electronics and custom flight control firmware" — no specific board or firmware project is named in any source found.

Cost

The raw foam board material itself is roughly $2; no total cost including electronics was found.
